## Clinically consequential claim

An awake adult ICU patient's report of breathing difficulty is meaningful clinical evidence. Visible inspiratory neck-muscle recruitment is an effort-related bedside observation. Neither is a quantitative measurement of respiratory-muscle work or, by itself, proof of a mechanism, diagnosis, severity, or treatment requirement. Meaningful breathing difficulty can coexist with apparently reassuring displayed gas-exchange/output values.

## Evidence

  - Exact location: abstract; the statement addresses clinical significance, detection, and quantification of dyspnoea in critically ill mechanically ventilated adults, reports that approximately 40% of invasively ventilated ICU patients report dyspnoea, and calls for identification and relief. M02 does not use its treatment sections.
- van Oosten JP, Akoumianaki E, Jonkman AH. *Monitoring respiratory muscles effort during mechanical ventilation.* Review. *Current Opinion in Critical Care.* 2025;31(1):12-20. DOI: [10.1097/MCC.0000000000001229](https://doi.org/10.1097/MCC.0000000000001229); PMCID `PMC11676600`.
  - Exact locations: **Physiology of breathing effort** (VT in assisted ventilation depends on assistance and patient effort; waveforms alone do not inform actual effort) and Table 1 (accessory/abdominal muscle use as a noninvasive first screening sign, no cut-off; distinct monitoring methods are listed for effort).

## Applicability and provenance

- ERS/ESICM directly addresses the adult mechanically ventilated ICU context. van Oosten is a critical-care review, not a diagnostic study validating neck-muscle recruitment as a numerical effort tool.
- The phrase “Breathing feels hard right now” is an authored synthetic patient report. It is not a validated severity score or specific dyspnoea instrument.
- “Can coexist” is a boundary against global reassurance, not a prevalence, causal, or prognostic claim.
- Classification: patient report is communicated/observed evidence; neck-muscle recruitment is observed; respiratory-muscle work is not measured in M02.
