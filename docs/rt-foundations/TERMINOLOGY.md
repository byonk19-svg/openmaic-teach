# Canonical Terminology

**Status:** normative product-language guidance. This document standardizes repository terminology; it does not establish clinical-ready content or replace source mapping and qualified RT review.

## Canonical vocabulary rule

When a term below applies, repository documentation, authoring prompts, UI copy, rubrics, and implementation notes use the preferred term unless a clinically necessary distinction requires different language. Avoided terms remain appropriate in quotations and external source titles, but not as product terminology when they blur a required distinction.

## Learning and evidence

| Preferred term | Meaning | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Supported conclusion** | The strongest conclusion visible evidence actually justifies, stated separately from plausible explanations and unresolved questions. | Blanket uncertainty, overclaim |
| **Breathing experience** | The patient's communicated experience of breathing; relevant evidence, not a quantified work-of-breathing measurement. | Measured effort, comfort score |
| **Effort-related observation** | A visible bedside finding, such as inspiratory neck-muscle recruitment, that warrants assessment without establishing a cause or quantified respiratory-muscle work. | Measured work of breathing, proven mechanism |
| **Gas moved per minute** | Calculated exhaled volume per minute for a stated, valid calculation interval; not a claim about CO2 clearance or breathing effort. | Adequate ventilation, clinical status |
| **Measurement applicability** | Whether legitimate evidence can answer the current question given its time, patient state, support conditions, source, and method. | Good data, bad data |
| **Independent retrieval attempt** | The learner's first unassisted response to a new reasoning task before targeted feedback, hints, or explanation exposure. | Retry, first quiz attempt |
| **Assisted revision** | A response made after targeted feedback or scaffolding. It supports learning but is not independent retrieval. | Independent mastery, fresh retrieval |
| **Reasoning checkpoint** | A learner response point that requires case-specific interpretation and the evidence appropriate to that objective before its explanation is unlocked. It may include mechanism, uncertainty, next assessment, reassessment, or coordination. | Trivia quiz, acknowledgement gate |

## Product and content boundaries

| Preferred term | Meaning | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **RT-relevant ICU context** | ICU context included only when it materially changes respiratory assessment, support, airway safety, escalation, or communication. | General ICU orientation, interdisciplinary scope expansion |
| **Generation completion** | Every planned scene is persisted according to the authoritative outline. | Learner completion, course complete |
| **Learner completion** | Every required reasoning checkpoint has a durable passing review. | Generated course, rendered course |
| **Clinical-ready content** | Source-traceable content with qualified RT review for the specific version and stated educational use. | AI-approved content, medically certified content |
| **Source record** | A versioned claim-level record connecting a consequential instructional assertion and every dependent asset to its source, limitations, derivation, and review state. | Module-level bibliography, citation-only approval |
| **Clinical claim** | An instructional assertion whose correctness could materially affect bedside interpretation, assessment, escalation, or understanding of respiratory care and therefore requires traceable support and review. | Any sentence with a citation |

## M01 application

M01 separates a valid gas-moved-per-minute calculation from conclusions about CO2 clearance and breathing experience. Case C teaches measurement applicability: an earlier gas is not bad evidence, but a support/time change can limit its ability to answer the current CO2 question.
