# Clinical Content, Evidence, and Review Policy

**Version:** 0.1 · 2026-09-15
**Type:** proposed product-governance policy. This file does not perform, substitute for, or attest to human clinical review.

## 1. Educational scope

The product is for private adult acute/ICU RT learning. It teaches interpretation, limitations, assessment, reassessment, communication, and escalation concepts. It is not a source of patient-specific orders or independent procedural/specialty authorization.

Clinical examples, rubrics, feedback, and simulations generated by an AI are drafts even when they are accurate-looking, cited, or technically functional. “Gold standard” in this pack means the instructional quality to aim for, not clinical approval.

## 2. Separate design decisions from clinical claims

| Type | Example | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Product/design choice | Use one focused feedback question; start with M01. | Label as product direction, not a guideline requirement or proven optimal method. |
| Synthetic case fact | The fictional interval contains 28 counted breaths. | Explicitly authored, internally consistent, and not represented as real-patient data. |
| Mathematical derivation | 28 breaths/min × 0.40 L/breath = 11.2 L/min. | Inputs, units, window, assumptions, and independent arithmetic check. |
| Clinical claim | A displayed volume does not itself establish a patient's effort. | Exact source mapping, applicability, limitations, and qualified clinical review. |
| Treatment or safety implication | A deterioration requires urgent reassessment/escalation. | Population/scope-specific support and qualified review; no implied procedural credential. |
| Model prediction | A control changes a modeled waveform or pressure. | Reviewed model, operating limits, dependent outputs, and precise provenance. |

## 3. Source rules

Prefer current official AARC, ATS/ERS/CHEST, SCCM, and ESICM guidance for questions those organizations address. Use other official bodies only where relevant. Retain older foundational documents when appropriate, but verify publication dates, updates, corrections, and continued applicability.

Use primary research for physiological and measurement questions that guidelines do not fully resolve. Manufacturer instructions are necessary for device-specific behavior. A guideline's authority does not extend to every unrelated claim in a module that cites it.

Record the actual section, recommendation, table, or page supporting the claim. Search snippets and abstracts can locate sources but are insufficient for detailed rules they do not contain. Do not invent quotations, section names, publication years, review dates, credentials, or replacement relationships. A website upload date is not a publication year.

Preserve recommendation strength, certainty, population, and setting. Distinguish a clinical practice guideline, official statement, expert consensus, diagnostic study, physiological experiment, and model. Do not convert an association into a causal mechanism or a conditional recommendation into a universal instruction.

Earlier research messages and their source IDs are leads, not an automatically verified library. If a title, date, URL, or replacement claim cannot be verified, mark it unresolved and keep dependent content out of clinical-ready use.

## 4. Required status workflow

| Status | Entry/exit condition | Sends it back |
|---|---|---|
| **Generated draft** | Intended use and synthetic assumptions are explicit; clinical review is not claimed. | Missing teaching scope, contradictory facts, invented evidence, or unsafe framing. |
| **Source-mapped** | All clinically consequential claims and dependent assets have appropriate support, exact locators, and recorded limits; unresolved essential claims block this status. | Inapplicable/insufficient sources, outdated guidance, ambiguous definitions, or unsupported extrapolation. |
| **Under qualified RT review** | A named reviewer with relevant adult ICU RT expertise evaluates the exact version, including prompts, answer keys, alternatives, feedback, and models. | Clinical errors, misleading implications, questionable realism, unfair rubric, or inadequate source mapping. |
| **Clinical-ready for the stated educational use** | The real review is approved for the exact scope/version; required clinical, source, technical, and release checks are complete. | Consequential edits, new applicable evidence, errors, device changes, failed QA, or expired required review. |

A revision returns the affected item to the appropriate earlier stage. Material carrying unresolved clinical meaning must not remain labeled clinical-ready while awaiting a future fix.

Private/public visibility is separate from clinical readiness. Default-off clinical-review features, if present, remain unchanged unless the current task explicitly authorizes otherwise. Documentation is not a permission grant, feature activation, approval record, or proof of runtime enforcement.

## 5. Reviewer and version requirements

Record the real reviewer identity, relevant credential, adult ICU experience/role, date, decision, scope, source set, and exact content version. Do not populate the owner's credentials or attestations automatically. When a claim reaches beyond RT expertise, obtain appropriate additional specialist review without suggesting the reviewer has approved unrelated content.

Use the existing repository's version/manifest mechanism if available. Do not create a parallel approval architecture for this pack. At minimum, a review must identify which exact explanations, cases, visuals, prompts, rubrics, feedback, and models it covers.

A change to a clinically consequential claim, number, case condition, answer criterion, model, or source applicability reopens affected review and dependent assets. Presentation-only edits still require a check that meaning, accessibility, and reveal behavior did not change. Do not blindly reuse an old approval.

## 7. Small evidence starter set for M01

**Checked for this pack:** 2026-09-15. This is a bounded starter set, not a systematic currency review of the entire curriculum. The M01 claim map states what remains unresolved.

### E01 — AARC patient-ventilator assessment

**Organization/authors:** AARC; Goodfellow LT, Miller AG, Varekojis SM, LaVita CJ, Glogowski JT, Hess DR.
**Title:** *AARC Clinical Practice Guideline: Patient-Ventilator Assessment.*
**Year/version:** 2024; *Respiratory Care* 69(8):1042–1054. DOI: 10.4187/respcare.12007.
**URL:** https://www.aarc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/patient-ventilator-assessment-aarc-cpg.pdf
**Inspected support:** Introduction, p.1043; Figure 1, p.1044, including visual inspection. The framework includes the patient examination, physiological data, airway, ventilator, humidification, and documentation.
**Use/limits:** Supports a patient-centered assessment framework, not this lesson's exact rubric or a proof about every minute-volume calculation. Full guideline accessed; no replacement identified in the limited AARC-catalog check. Qualified review of the lesson remains unperformed.

### E02 — Primary effort-measurement study

**Authors:** Albani F, Fusina F, Ciabatti G, et al.
**Title:** *Flow Index accurately identifies breaths with low or high inspiratory effort during pressure support ventilation.*
**Year/version:** 2021; *Critical Care* 25:427. DOI: 10.1186/s13054-021-03855-4.
**URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13054-021-03855-4
**Inspected support:** Abstract Methods/Results and full-text article accessed. In this 24-subject analysis, usual breathing-pattern variables did not separate the study's effort groups as the investigated effort indices did.
**Use/limits:** Primary, mode-specific evidence supporting caution about inferring effort from breathing pattern. It is not a guideline, an assessment of this curriculum, or a universal minute-volume rule. Do not teach its research cutoffs, require Flow Index, or extrapolate its diagnostic performance to every device/mode.

### E03 — Primary dead-space physiology study

**Authors:** Hinkson CR, Benson MS, Stephens LM, Deem S.
**Title:** *The Effects of Apparatus Dead Space on PaCO2 in Patients Receiving Lung-Protective Ventilation.*
**Year/version:** 2006; *Respiratory Care* 51(10):1140–1144. DOI: 10.4187/respcare.06511140.
**URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.4187/respcare.06511140
**Inspected support:** Publisher abstract Methods/Results; seven adult ARDS/acute-lung-injury patients, with configured tidal volume and rate held constant during circuit comparisons. PaCO2 changed with apparatus configuration.
**Use/limits:** Older physiological evidence illustrating why configured breath delivery is not a complete CO2 assessment. The complete paper and generalized physiological explanation require further review. This source does not establish that measured minute ventilation was identical in all conditions, and it does not authorize removing humidification or circuit components in the lesson.

### E04 — Official dyspnea statement

**Organization/authors:** ATS; Parshall MB, Schwartzstein RM, Adams L, et al.
**Title:** *An Official American Thoracic Society Statement: Update on the Mechanisms, Assessment, and Management of Dyspnea.*
**Year/version:** 2012; *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine* 185(4):435–452. DOI: 10.1164/rccm.201111-2042ST.
**URLs:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22336677/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5448624/
**Located support:** Definition and patient-reported symptom framing; source record and indexed primary-text excerpt located. Full relevant sections must be retrieved and checked during source mapping.
**Use/limits:** Supports treating breathing discomfort as a reported symptom, not as a direct numerical effort measurement. Retained as a foundational lead; this pack does not assert an exhaustive current replacement check or use its treatment sections as current ICU protocols.

### E05 — AARC source-discovery catalog

**Organization:** AARC.
**Title:** *Clinical Practice Guidelines* catalog; living page, accessed 2026-09-15.
**URL:** https://www.aarc.org/resource/clinical-practice-guidelines/
**Use/limits:** Source discovery and a limited update check only. Listing a historical document does not verify every recommendation or imply current endorsement of every clinical example.

## 8. Open source/review work before M01 release

Complete exact mapping of the general physiological explanation connecting total volume, effective gas-exchanging ventilation, and CO2 production. Complete the dyspnea section review and review the distinction between symptom report, observed effort, and quantified muscle work. Confirm the proposed next-evidence examples in the lesson with a qualified adult ICU RT. Do not fill those gaps by treating one broad AARC citation as blanket support.

The arithmetic can be checked now and the instructional design can be audited now. Those completed tasks do not remove the remaining clinical-review requirements.

## 9. Additional safeguards

Use synthetic cases by default. Do not put identifiable patient information, protected clinical screenshots, secrets, or credentials in the repository. Source citations do not grant permission to copy proprietary images, tables, videos, device assets, or instruments; verify rights separately and prefer original reviewed illustrations.

Review surveillance may be scheduled by the owner—for example, quarterly source checks and annual formal re-review—but those intervals are product proposals, not society requirements or automations created by this document. Important new evidence, corrections, device updates, safety reports, or consequential content edits trigger earlier review.
