# Product North Star

**Version:** 0.1 · 2026-09-15
**Type:** proposed product and instructional-design direction, not evidence of clinical effectiveness.

## The product in one sentence

A private, RT-first learning environment that helps practicing adult acute/ICU respiratory therapists explain a bedside situation, distinguish evidence from inference, and identify a justified assessment, reassessment, or communication need.

## The experience we are aiming for

An RT opens a short module and recognizes the kind of question that comes up during a shift. A clean visual makes one underlying relationship easier to see. A worked example shows how to use that relationship without burying it in a lecture. The RT then interprets a new case before seeing its explanation.

A partial answer receives a useful follow-up, not a scolding and not the full answer. A thoughtful alternative passes when it fits the evidence. New evidence sometimes resolves uncertainty and sometimes creates a different concern. The learner leaves with a more useful way to think, not merely a memorized phrase.

**The desired feeling:** “I understand why that distinction matters, I can explain it, and I know what information would change my interpretation.”

## What success looks like in a learner response

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Names a familiar number, setting, pattern, or diagnosis. | Explains what the supplied evidence supports. |
| Treats a valid calculation as a complete assessment. | Uses the calculation and limits the conclusion appropriately. |
| Requests “more information” without a purpose. | Identifies a particular uncertainty and evidence that could resolve it. |
| Repeats the previous answer despite a changed case. | Updates the interpretation when relevant evidence changes. |
| Jumps directly to an intervention. | Establishes an assessment rationale before discussing action. |
| Performs only after seeing the explanation. | Can apply the distinction to a fresh task with assistance tracked honestly. |

These are educational behaviors to look for. They are not a claim that this product establishes bedside competence or improves patient outcomes.

## Three promises we can actually keep

**Teach a usable mental model.** “Foundations” means making important relationships clear, not assuming the learner needs an RT-school survey. Define a term when it matters, show the relationship, and get to an authentic reasoning task.

**Treat the learner fairly.** Supply enough information to answer the stated question. Explain what will be assessed. Accept concise, clinically defensible wording. Do not require the learner to guess hidden case facts, author intent, or a keyword password.

**Keep the evidence honest.** Distinguish observations, settings, measurements, calculations, and models. Show what is missing without inventing it. Sources and qualified review establish the content's clinical status; polished presentation does not.

## The common learning arc

**Simple visual explanation → worked example → independent retrieval or decision → targeted feedback → spaced practice or transfer.**

A short prediction pause may precede a worked explanation. That is supported instruction, not an independent mastery test. An independent case must change the evidence meaningfully, not just the name or arithmetic.

Later modules can be more complex. The arc does not require identical scene counts, mandatory simulations, or a fixed number of typed gates in every module. Constraints attached to an existing artifact are not automatically universal product rules.

## What this is not

| Not the destination | Better direction |
|---|---|
| A narrated textbook with a quiz at the end. | A short causal explanation that is used immediately. |
| Ten advanced ventilator puzzles. | Broad RT reasoning that progressively adds complexity. |
| A clinical checklist that requires every possible assessment in every answer. | A focused question with a small, explicit rubric. |
| A device simulator that predicts patient benefit from unvalidated sliders. | A reviewed simple model or clearly authored case transition. |
| A course that rewards skepticism about all measurements. | A course that rewards appropriate confidence and specific uncertainty. |
| Autonomous treatment advice or independent specialty credentialing. | Recognition, interpretation, coordinated assessment, and escalation awareness. |
| Exam preparation, CE credit, or guaranteed professional competence. | Educational practice with accurately described completion and review status. |

## RT-centered does not mean ventilator-only

The patient remains central. Airway integrity, gas exchange, effort, perfusion, medication effects, recovery, communication, and patient goals can materially change the respiratory question. Bring that context in when it matters; do not turn the product into a compressed medical-specialty curriculum.

The first sequence is adult acute/ICU only. Pediatric, neonatal, obstetric, and other specialized tracks require their own source mapping and review. Existing NICU interests do not expand this release.

## Tradeoffs when deciding what to build

| Prefer | Over |
|---|---|
| A clear explanation and realistic question. | More content volume or animation. |
| One excellent pilot. | Ten generated modules with the same teaching defect. |
| A static, accurate diagram. | An interactive but unvalidated physiological prediction. |
| A specific feedback question. | Generic praise, a dense rubric dump, or a numerical “reasoning score.” |
| One maintained rule in its owning document. | Repeated instructions scattered across handoffs. |
| The smallest authorized change that completes the task. | Adjacent architecture work that does not improve the current lesson. |
| A stated source/review gap. | An impressive but unsupported clinical-ready label. |

## Current product milestone

**M01: a correct number does not automatically justify a broader conclusion.**

The exemplar targets the tempting claim that calculated minute ventilation of 11.2 L/min proves adequate CO2 clearance and acceptable breathing effort. It must also prevent the opposite error: assuming inadequacy or dismissing the calculation as useless.

A fresh case must make blanket uncertainty insufficient by supplying additional relevant evidence. The learner should use that evidence without overstating it.

## Evidence of progress

Judge the current work by whether a reviewer can point to where the idea is taught, used, independently assessed, and transferred; whether feedback handles different errors differently; whether clinically consequential claims are traceable; and whether the rendered experience matches its storyboard.

Completion rate, time on task, hint use, or correctness may describe educational activity when actually measured. None is a clinical-competence score. Do not invent analytics or build a measurement platform before a concrete product need is authorized.

## The deciding question

> Does this change help an RT make a better-supported interpretation of the patient, or does it only make the product look more elaborate?

If a proposed feature does not improve a specified learning objective, repair a demonstrated defect, or meet a required safety/review condition, it is not part of the current milestone.
